--- v4.1/4.5: no-fit reasons (2) ---
- Forklift preventive maintenance: Forklift preventive maintenance is fundamentally a hands-on physical task. The core pain points—physical inspection, hands-on repair work, and scheduling constraints around warehouse operations—cannot be addressed by information-processing AI. While the company does have maintenance logs, equipment manuals, and parts inventory data, the bottlenecks are not in data processing but in the physical work itself and operational scheduling. Parts availability delays are a supply-chain coordination issue, not an information extraction or classification problem. AI might help with scheduling optimization or parts forecasting at the margins, but neither directly addresses the stated pain points of inspection and repair work, and neither would generate realistic time savings on the 45-minute occurrence itself. This is a case where the process is primarily physical labor, not data-driven work.
- Annual performance review drafting and calibration: This process involves evaluation of workers' performance and behaviour, which falls squarely into EU AI Act HIGH-RISK territory (Annex III, biometric categorisation for performance monitoring). Additionally, any AI system standardising or augmenting performance review narratives risks introducing algorithmic bias into employment decisions with direct legal and ethical consequences (promotion, termination, compensation). While some downstream tasks (e.g. extracting summary statistics for a human calibrator) might be lower-risk, the core pain points—inconsistent manager write-ups and calibration preparation—are asking for AI to either generate or standardise evaluative content about individual employees. This cannot be responsibly addressed without bespoke high-risk governance that is beyond standard AI tooling. The sensitivity of personal performance data and the legal stakes of employment decisions make this a poor candidate for rapid, off-the-shelf AI intervention. The firm's HR and legal teams should own this decision; it is not appropriate for this assessment to propose a use case.
